Abstract

Predicting motion geometry, not coordinates

Self-supervised learning has become a strong paradigm for skeleton-based action recognition, but the choice of prediction target remains a central limitation. Masked modeling methods that reconstruct raw joint coordinates emphasize low-level detail and are sensitive to noise, while non-reconstruction variants predict learned latent features that improve semantics but provide little explicit motion structure.

We propose Path-JEPA, a self-supervised predictive framework that uses path signatures as the target representation for skeleton sequences. Instead of predicting coordinates or abstract embeddings, Path-JEPA predicts latent representations of continuous-time, multi-scale geometric descriptors computed over joint, edge, and chain paths. These targets encode displacement, signed area, and higher-order interactions — a geometry-grounded representation of human motion that is naturally robust to temporal resampling and frame-rate variation.

To make such targets effective within a JEPA framework, we introduce signature-augmented masking, which propagates joint-space masks to all dependent signature tokens and forces the model to infer missing motion geometry from broader anatomical and temporal context. Across NTU RGB+D 60, NTU RGB+D 120, and PKU-MMD, Path-JEPA learns stronger representations than prior masked-prediction methods, reaching state-of-the-art on multiple downstream tasks while remaining markedly more robust under irregular sampling.

Contributions

Four moving parts

Framework

Path-JEPA

A JEPA-based SSL framework for skeleton action recognition that predicts embeddings derived from path-signature features rather than raw coordinates.

Tokenization

Multi-scale relational signatures

Signature tokens over joint, pair, and kinematic-chain paths, capturing relational structure and temporal abstraction over continuously interpolated motion.

Masking

Signature-augmented masking

Joint masking is propagated to every dependent signature token, enforcing leak-free structured prediction over motion geometry across the token hierarchy.

Results

Robust, transferable features

Improved linear evaluation, fine-tuning, semi-supervised, and cross-dataset performance — with strong robustness to temporal resampling and irregular sampling.
